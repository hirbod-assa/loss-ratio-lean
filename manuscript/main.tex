\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This paper has two aims of equal weight. The first is mathematical: to characterize the loss ratio as a risk
indicator by necessary and sufficient conditions, as Cherny and Madan characterized performance measures such as
the Sharpe ratio and the RAROC. The second is methodological: the result was obtained by putting AI to the test on
this problem, with a panel of AI agents working under the author's direction, and the paper reports this process
in full and proposes a standard for reporting AI-assisted proofs in mathematical risk management.

The loss ratio $\LR(X)$ of an insurance book $X$ is the ratio of a loss measure $\rho(X)$, such as the expected
loss or a tail measure, to a premium $\Pi(X)$. We show that both parts can be \emph{implied} by the loss ratio
itself. Along the cash line $c\mapsto\LR(X+c)$, obtained by adding sure losses $c$ to the book, there is exactly
one point where the ratio stops being finite; its location is the \emph{implied premium}, the sure payment at which
the book changes hands. The \emph{implied risk measure} then follows from the accounting identity loss $=$ loss
ratio $\times$ premium, and it is visible even where the ratio explodes, as the residue of the singularity. With
this reading, four natural axioms on the loss ratio -- a single non-finite point on each cash line, a loss ratio of
one for sure losses, premium-weighted comonotone pooling, and law invariance -- together with continuity
(respectively monotonicity) of the implied premium and risk measure characterize the loss ratios $\LR=I_h/I_g$,
where $I_h,I_g$ are signed Choquet integrals with distortion functions $h,g$ (respectively distortion premia), with
$(h,g)$ unique. The unit-slope M\"obius form of the cash lines, comonotonic additivity of the implied premium, and
the association with a family of risk measures are derived rather than assumed. We show that no non-trivial loss ratio is monotone, show by counterexamples that none of the axioms
except the one defining the implied premium can be dropped, and state candidly which parts of the result are
classical.

The work used Claude (Anthropic) as a main assistant and to run panels of AI agents that surveyed the literature,
proposed and ranked candidate theorems, drafted proofs and counterexamples, and reviewed the proofs adversarially;
ChatGPT (OpenAI) and Gemini (Google) were used for an independent review of the proofs and for summaries of cited
works. All decisions on content were the author's, and the central idea, the implied premium and risk measure,
arose from the author's objections to AI-drafted formulations. We report the organization of the work, how the
knowledge base was assembled, the errors in AI-generated mathematics that were found and corrected, the guardrails,
a formal check in Lean of the steps specific to loss ratios, and what has not been verified, and distil these into
a checklist for reporting AI-assisted proofs.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} loss ratio; implied premium; implied risk measure; axiomatic characterization;
