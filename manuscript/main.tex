\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Mathematical results in risk management and actuarial science are increasingly obtained with substantial help from
AI, from a chatbot that suggests a lemma to panels of AI agents that propose, prove and attack theorems. The
declarations of AI use that journals now require do not tell a reader which parts of such a result were exposed to
AI error and which checks they passed. This paper proposes a standard for reporting AI-assisted proofs: nine items,
applied in proportion to how heavily AI was used, each marked by whether an editor or reviewer can verify it, and
designed so that honest reporting is in the author's interest. Formal verification in a proof assistant and a
public repository of the proof records are encouraged rather than required.

The standard is applied to a case study, carried out with a panel of AI agents under the author's direction: an
axiomatic characterization of the loss ratio as a risk indicator, in the spirit of Cherny and Madan's theory of
performance measures. The premium and the risk measure are implied by the loss ratio itself, the premium as the
point at which the ratio stops being finite along the cash line $c\mapsto\LR(X+c)$. Axioms that refer only to values
of the loss ratio -- a non-finite point on each cash line, comonotone pooling of books with zero premium, law
invariance, and continuity (respectively monotonicity) of the sure amounts that remove the premium and the loss of a
book -- characterize the loss ratios $I_h/I_g$, ratios of signed Choquet integrals (respectively distortion premia),
with $(h,g)$ unique; none of the axioms can be dropped, and no axiom on finite values alone can replace the pooling
axiom. The report of the case study records the organization of the agents, the seventeen errors in AI-generated
mathematics that were found, the checks applied, a formal check in Lean, the revisions prompted by readers, and
what has not been verified. We compare the approach with recent large-scale multi-agent efforts in mathematics.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} AI-assisted proofs; reporting standards; large language models; multi-agent systems;
formal verification; Lean; loss ratio; implied premium; axiomatic characterization; signed Choquet integral;
comonotonic additivity.
