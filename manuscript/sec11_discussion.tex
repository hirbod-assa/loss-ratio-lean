\section{Discussion and open problems}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{The standard.}
The proposed standard asks authors of AI-heavy work for information that a reviewer can use, in proportion to how
heavily AI was used, and it separates what can be verified from what must be declared. Several questions are open.
Where exactly the boundaries between the tiers lie will become clear only from use. How reviewers may themselves use
AI, and whether the verification of formal statements against the paper can be shared between author, reviewer and
the community, are questions for journals. The standard also presupposes that formal verification is feasible for
part of the work; for risk management this depends on libraries of formalized probability and Choquet integration
that do not yet exist. Finally, the comparison with large-scale efforts suggests that the design principles of
multi-agent work, and the records needed to report it, are largely independent of scale, but this is based on one
case study.

\paragraph{The case study.}
A loss ratio carries its own premium and its own risk measure. The premium is the price point of the cash line,
the sure payment at which the book changes hands, and the risk measure is loss ratio times premium, visible even
where the ratio explodes as the residue of the singularity. With this reading, axioms on the values of the loss
ratio alone, whose substance is that the underwriting results of comonotone books of zero premium add, together with
law invariance and regularity of two sure amounts, characterize the loss ratios that are ratios of two Choquet-type
functionals. The characterization is sharp, and the pair is unique. At the level of representations it is a
transfer of the theory of \citet{yaari1987}, \citet{schmeidler1986}, \citet{wyp1997} and \citet{www2020}; at the
level of the loss ratio it is new, and it comes with facts that have no single-functional counterpart: no non-trivial
loss ratio is monotone (Proposition~\ref{prop:F2}), the association with a family of risk measures holds exactly
off $\{\rho=\Pi=0\}$ and is signed (Corollary~\ref{cor:assoc} in the Supplement), and no axiom on finite values of
the ratio can replace the pooling axiom (Proposition~\ref{prop:nofinite}).

\paragraph{Open problems.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item \emph{Beyond comonotone pooling.} The most promising direction is to drop \axR{} in favour of an axiom
  that allows diversification, so that the implied premium and risk measure are convex or subadditive rather
  than comonotonic additive. The implied premium and the implied risk measure are defined without \axR{} only
  partly (the premium needs only \axP; the accounting reading of $\rho$ needs pooling with sure losses), and the
  resulting class, a ratio of Kusuoka-type functionals, has no characterization yet. Replacing comonotonic
  additivity by convexity leads to suprema of signed Choquet integrals \citep{liu2020}, so no single pair $(h,g)$
  and no uniqueness should be expected.
\item \emph{Weaker cardinal axioms.} Can \axR{} be replaced by the mediant inequality plus a supplementary
  condition? By Section~\ref{sec:necessity}, such a condition must involve non-finite values of $\LR$. The inequality implies additivity for comonotone pairs strictly on the same side of break-even, but
  not for pairs straddling $1$ or lying in the break-even set.
\item \emph{Order properties of $\LR$.} \axRM{} is an order property of $\LR$, but of the sure amounts at which
  its cash lines become non-finite or vanish. Whether it is equivalent to an order property of the values of $\LR$ alone is open; by
  Proposition~\ref{prop:F2} it cannot be pointwise monotonicity.
\item \emph{Loadings on sure losses.} We conjecture that an analogue of Theorem~\ref{thm:A} holds for ratios
  $\bracket{I_h}{\kappa I_g}$ with a known loading $\kappa>0$, which would admit constant loss ratios $\ne1$.
\item \emph{Quasi-convexity.} Necessity of the conditions in Corollary~\ref{cor:shape}(d) is open.
\item \emph{Actuarial domain.} The axioms for nonnegative losses (Section~\ref{sec:actuarial} of the Supplement)
  are not yet stated on values of the loss ratio alone. Whether (A4) imposed only on pairs with $X,Y,X+Y\notin N_0$ suffices is open; a
  truncation-continuity (Greco-type) version of the signed tier on the cone is not known.
\item \emph{Unbounded losses.} Extensions to $L^p$ and Orlicz spaces via \citet{www2020b} are not treated.
\end{enumerate}
