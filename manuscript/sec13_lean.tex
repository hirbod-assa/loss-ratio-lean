\subsection{Formal checking in Lean}\label{sec:collab-lean}

Lean~4 \citep{demoura2021} is an interactive proof assistant in which every proof is checked by a small trusted
program, the kernel; Mathlib \citep{mathlib2020} is its community library of formalized mathematics. A successful
check establishes that the formal statements follow from the formal definitions. It does not establish that they say
what the paper says, which a human reader must check, and it says nothing about results that enter as hypotheses or
are not formalized. A formal check is useful here because the proofs were drafted with AI, and because the steps
specific to loss ratios (Lemmas~\ref{lem:premium}--\ref{lem:LI}) are elementary but delicate, with case distinctions
at $\Pi(X)=0$, at break-even books and at the price point, which is where informal checking is most likely to miss a
case.

The formalization works in an abstract setting: positions form an additive commutative group with an embedding of
the sure losses, a relation ``comonotone'' and a relation ``equal in law'' with the few algebraic properties that the
proofs use, and a loss ratio takes values in $\R\cup\{\mathtt{none}\}$, where \texttt{none} stands for a non-finite
value. Properties \axP, \axN{} and \axCM{} and Axiom \axLI{} are formalized as stated in Section~\ref{sec:axioms}; the
Lean text is given in Section~\ref{supp:lean} of the Supplement. The file has about 500 lines with 10 definitions and
26 lemmas and theorems, and Table~\ref{tab:lean} lists the results formalized. The theorem \texttt{main\_conditional}
takes the representation step \citep[Thm.~1]{www2020} as a hypothesis and derives from \axP, \axN, \axCM{} and \axLI{}
that $\LR=\bracket{\rho}{\Pi}$ everywhere. Not formalized are the representation step itself
(Lemmas~\ref{lem:cashlip}--\ref{lem:rep}), which would require a theory of Choquet integrals that Mathlib does not
provide; the fact that $L^\infty$ is an instance of the abstract setting; the examples and counterexamples; and the
present axioms \axPe{} and \axR, their equivalence with \axP, \axN{} and \axCM{} (Lemma~\ref{lem:R}), and the
regularity axioms in the form of Lemma~\ref{lem:offsets}.

The Lean file was written by the main assistant and compiled on the author's computer; the assistant read each
build log and corrected proof steps only, and the third build succeeded. The final file contains no \texttt{sorry},
\texttt{admit} or added \texttt{axiom}; \texttt{\#print axioms} for each theorem in Table~\ref{tab:lean} lists only
\texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice} and \texttt{Quot.sound}; the final build started from an empty build
directory; and the compiled file is identical to the published one. The formal statements were compared with the
paper by the assistant, and this comparison is to be repeated by the author. The details of these checks and a timed
record of the build-and-repair loop are in Section~\ref{supp:lean} of the Supplement; the Lean project (Lean and
Mathlib version~4.36.0-rc1) is in the repository with instructions for rebuilding it.
