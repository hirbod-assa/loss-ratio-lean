\section{The case study: question and related literature}\label{sec:lit}

Sections~\ref{sec:lit} to~\ref{sec:necessity} present the mathematical case study. The question is which maps
from losses to $\R\cup\{\infty,\star\}$ are loss ratios $\rho/\Pi$ of a risk measure and a premium of Choquet type,
characterized by axioms on the ratio alone. Three features distinguish it from the classical characterizations of
premium principles and risk measures \citep{yaari1987,wyp1997,schmeidler1986,www2020}. First, the object is a ratio,
while those results concern a single functional. Second, the ratio does not determine its numerator and
denominator: on positive losses, $(\rho,\Pi)$ and $(\psi\rho,\psi\Pi)$ give the same ratio for every positive
functional $\psi$. Third, the ratio is not monotone (Proposition~\ref{prop:F2}), so the theory of acceptability
indices of \citet{chernymadan2009}, which rests on monotonicity, does not apply. Table~\ref{tab:littable} in the
Supplement summarizes the differences.

\paragraph{Premia, Choquet integrals and signed Choquet integrals.}
The axiomatic theory of insurance premia starts from properties of a single functional: translation invariance,
no unjustified loading \citep{gerber1974}, monotonicity, law invariance and additivity for comonotonic risks. The
dual theory of \citet{yaari1987} leads to the distortion premium $\int g(P(X>x))\,dx$, introduced as a premium
principle by \citet{wang1996} and characterized by \citet{wyp1997}; the backbone is the theorem of
\citet{schmeidler1986} that monotone comonotonic additive functionals are Choquet integrals \citep[see
also][]{greco1982,denneberg1994}. Law-invariant comonotonic coherent risk measures are mixtures of expected
shortfalls \citep{adeh1999,delbaen2002,kusuoka2001,jst2006,folmerschied2016}. Without monotonicity, comonotonic
additive functionals of bounded variation are signed Choquet integrals \citep{msm1994,cmmm2012,cmmm2015}, and
\citet{www2020} prove the law-invariant version used here, together with the characterization of convexity;
\citet{www2020b} extend it beyond $L^\infty$. All of these results characterize one functional by axioms on that
functional. We are not aware of an if-and-only-if characterization of a ratio of two Choquet integrals by axioms on
the ratio.

\paragraph{Performance ratios and acceptability indices.}
\citet{chernymadan2009} characterize monotone, quasi-concave, scale-invariant acceptability indices by increasing
families of coherent risk measures, recovered by the cash-shift formula $\rho_x(X)=\inf\{c:\alpha(X+c)\ge x\}$;
see also \citet{bcz2014,bbn2014,drapeaukupper2013} and, for gain--loss and reward--risk ratios,
\citet{bernardoledoit2000,biaginipinar2013,ck2013}. \citet{hlw2025} characterize diversification quotients, the
closest if-and-only-if result of level-crossing type. The cash-shift formula is the model for our reading of the
premium and the risk measure from cash lines. The differences are of direction, since numerator and denominator of a
loss ratio both increase with the loss and the ratio is not monotone; of cardinality, since the loss ratio obeys an
accounting identity under pooling; and of object, since we characterize the ratio together with its numerator and
denominator.

\paragraph{Implicit functionals and ratio representations.}
Orlicz premia \citep{hg1982,brg2008}, PELVE \citep{liwang2023,fiorirg2023}, return and star-shaped risk measures
\citep{blrg2018,castagnoli2022} are defined implicitly by level crossings; the literature establishes existence and
uniqueness but not ratio representations. In decision theory, weighted-utility preferences are represented by a
ratio of linear functionals \citep{chew1983}, while betweenness yields only implicit representations
\citep{dekel1986,fishburn1983}; the same distinction separates a weighted pooling identity from a mediant inequality
in our setting (Example~\ref{ex:cube}). Ratios of expectations are elicitable \citep{gneiting2011}, a statistical
statement without pooling or cash shifts.
