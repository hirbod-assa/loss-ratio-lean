\section{Independence of the axioms}\label{sec:necessity}

For each of the axioms \axPe, \axR, \axLI, \axRC{} and \axRM{} we give a loss ratio that satisfies the
remaining axioms and is not of the form in Theorem~\ref{thm:A} or~\ref{thm:B}. The constructions and their verifications are in Section~\ref{supp:witnesses} of the Supplement;
Table~\ref{tab:witnesses} summarizes them.

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}l>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{4.6cm}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Dropped} & \textbf{Witness} & \textbf{Why it is a witness}\\
\midrule
\axPe & $\LR\equiv1$
 & No value is non-finite; \axR, \axLI, \axRC, \axRM{} hold vacuously.\\
\axR & $\bracket{\max(I_{\sqrt t},I_{\min(1,1.5t)})}{\E}$; also $\psi\circ\bracket{I_h}{I_g}$, $\psi(t)=t^3$ (Example~\ref{ex:cube}), and $\bracket{\Pi'}{\Pi'}$
 & Implied risk measure (resp.\ premium) is cash additive but not comonotonic additive; the cube has poles of order three.\\
\axLI & $\bracket{\E_Q}{\E}$, $Q\ne P$ with bounded density
 & All other axioms hold; $\E_Q$ is not law invariant.\\
\axRC{} (Thm.~\ref{thm:A}) & $\bracket{I_h}{\E}$ with $h$ continuous, not of bounded variation
 & Uniform continuity fails; $h$ is not of bounded variation.\\
\axRM{} (Thm.~\ref{thm:B}) & $\bracket{I_h}{\E}$, $h(t)=t+\tfrac14\sin2\pi t$
 & $h$ is not monotone, so no distortion pair represents it.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\caption{Witnesses for the axioms of Theorems~\ref{thm:A} and~\ref{thm:B}. Here $\Pi'=\max(I_{\sqrt t},I_{\min(1,1.5t)})$.}\label{tab:witnesses}
\end{table}

Three of the witnesses carry a lesson. A loading on sure losses, $\LR=\bracket{I_h}{\kappa I_g}$ with $\kappa>0$,
$\kappa\ne1$, satisfies every axiom except \axR, which therefore contains the normalization on sure losses. The cube
$\psi\circ\bracket{I_h}{I_g}$ with $\psi(t)=t^3$ has the same price points and zeros as $\bracket{I_h}{I_g}$ and
satisfies the mediant inequality, yet it violates \axR: ordinal, betweenness-type information determines a loss ratio
only up to an increasing transformation, as for \citet{chew1983} and \citet{dekel1986}, and the cardinal axiom \axR{}
cannot be avoided. And the ratio $\bracket{\Pi'}{\Pi'}$ equals $1$ wherever it is finite and fails \axR{} only through
the clause that a pool of unpriced books is unpriced, so that no axiom on finite values of $\LR$ can replace \axR.
Some regularity is also indispensable: without \axRC{} and \axRM{} there is a witness built from a Hamel basis, which
uses the axiom of choice; all the other witnesses are constructive.
