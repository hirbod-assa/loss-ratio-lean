\section{Independence of the axioms}\label{sec:necessity}

For each of the axioms \axPe, \axR, \axLI, \axRC{} and \axRM{} we give a loss ratio that satisfies the
remaining axioms and is not of the form in Theorem~\ref{thm:A} or~\ref{thm:B}. Two facts shorten the verifications.
First, by Proposition~\ref{prop:RPNCM}, a ratio satisfies \axPe{} and \axR{} exactly when it has the properties
\axP, \axN{} and \axCM. Second, for a ratio $\bracket{A}{B}$ of cash-additive functionals the sure amounts that
make $X$ unpriced and loss-free are $B(X)$ and $A(X)$ (Section~\ref{sec:reg}), so \axRC{} and \axRM{} reduce to
continuity and monotonicity of $A$ and $B$. Table~\ref{tab:witnesses} summarizes the examples.

\begin{example}[Independence of \axPe]
The constant ratio $\LR\equiv1$ takes no non-finite value, so \axPe{} fails. Axioms \axR, \axRC{} and \axRM{} hold
vacuously, since their hypotheses require a non-finite value or a zero, and \axLI{} is clear. It is not of the form
in Theorem~\ref{thm:A}: every such ratio is non-finite at $X=0$, since $I_g(0)=0$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[A loading on sure losses violates \axR]
Fix $\kappa>0$, $\kappa\neq1$, and let $\LR=\bracket{I_h}{\kappa I_g}$ with distortions $h,g$. The cash line of
$X$ is $(I_h(X)+c)/(\kappa(I_g(X)+c))$, non-finite only at $c=-I_g(X)$, so \axPe{} and \axP{} hold with $\Pi=I_g$,
and with these weights the pooling identity \axCM{} holds because $I_h$ and $I_g$ are comonotonic additive. But
$\LR(c)=1/\kappa\ne1$, so \axN{} fails and, by Proposition~\ref{prop:RPNCM}, so does \axR. Directly: for unpriced
$X$, $s(\LR(X+s)-1)=(I_h(X)+s(1-\kappa))/\kappa$ depends on $s$. The normalization on sure losses is therefore built
into \axR: it is what excludes loadings on sure losses. (We conjecture, but have not proved, that an analogue of
Theorem~\ref{thm:A} with a known loading $\kappa$ holds with $\LR=\bracket{I_h}{\kappa I_g}$.)
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Ordinal information is not enough]\label{ex:cube}
Let $h\ne g$ be distortions and $\LR'=\psi\circ\bracket{I_h}{I_g}$ with $\psi(t)=t^3$ and $\psi$ fixing the
non-finite values. Since $\psi$ maps $\R$ to $\R$, $\LR'$ has the same price points as $\bracket{I_h}{I_g}$, so
\axPe{} and \axP{} hold with $\Pi=I_g$; \axN{} holds because $1^3=1$; \axLI{} holds, and so does the mediant inequality,
because $\psi$ is increasing. But $\LR'$ is not of the form in Theorem~\ref{thm:A}: at a book $X$ with
$I_h(X)\neq I_g(X)$ (for instance an indicator at a point where $h\ne g$), the cash line
$\big((I_h(X)+c)/(I_g(X)+c)\big)^3$ has a pole of order three, while every cash line in Theorem~\ref{thm:A} has a
simple pole. Hence \axCM{} fails, and so does \axR{} by Proposition~\ref{prop:RPNCM}. Since $\psi$ maps $0$ to $0$, $1$
to $1$ and only $1$ to $1$, and keeps the non-finite values, the sure amounts that make a book unpriced or loss-free
are the same for $\LR'$ as for $\bracket{I_h}{I_g}$, namely $I_g(X)$ and $I_h(X)$; so \axRC{} and \axRM{} hold as well,
and the example satisfies every axiom except \axR. Betweenness-type, ordinal information determines the loss ratio only up to an
increasing transformation, as for \citet{chew1983} and \citet{dekel1986}; the cardinal axiom \axR{} cannot be
avoided.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Independence of \axR]\label{ex:indepR}
Let $\LR=\bracket{\rho}{\E}$ with $\rho=\max(I_{\sqrt t},I_{\min(1,1.5t)})$. \axPe{} and \axP{} hold with $\Pi=\E$, \axN{}
holds, and the implied risk measure is $\rho$, a maximum of cash-additive maps; \axLI, \axRM{} and \axRC{} hold. Take
$E\subset B$ with $P(B)=0.5$, $P(E)=0.1$ and $X=\1_B+\1_E$. Then $\rho(\1_B)=\max(0.707,0.75)=0.75$ and
$\rho(\1_E)=\max(0.316,0.15)=0.316$, with sum $1.066$, while $\rho(X)=\max(0.707+0.316,\ 0.75+0.15)=1.023$. So $\rho$
is not comonotonic additive and \axCM{} fails by Lemma~\ref{lem:comon}(a); hence \axR{} fails.

The ratio $\bracket{\Pi'}{\Pi'}$ with $\Pi'=\rho$ shows more: it equals $1$ wherever it is finite, and satisfies
\axPe, \axLI, \axRC{} and \axRM{} (both sure amounts equal $\Pi'(X)$, which is monotone and $1$-Lipschitz). It fails
\axR{} only through the clause that a pool of unpriced books is unpriced: for the books above, $\1_B-\Pi'(\1_B)$ and
$\1_E-\Pi'(\1_E)$ are unpriced but their sum is not: its premium is $1.023-1.066=-0.043$. Hence no axiom that
depends on $\LR$ only through its finite values can replace \axR{} (Proposition~\ref{prop:nofinite}).
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Independence of \axLI]
Let $Z=dQ/dP$ be bounded, non-constant, $Z\ge0$ and $\E Z=1$, and $\LR=\bracket{\E_Q}{\E}$. \axP{} holds with
$\Pi=\E$, \axN{} holds, \axCM{} holds by Lemma~\ref{lem:comon}(b) because $\E_Q$ and $\E$ are additive, so \axPe{} and
\axR{} hold, and \axRM, \axRC{} hold. But $\E_Q(\1_A)=Q(A)$ is not a function of $P(A)$, so the implied risk measure is not of the form
$I_h$. Without \axLI{} the class is exactly that of Theorem~\ref{thm:A0}.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Independence of \axRC{} in Theorem~\ref{thm:A}]
Let $h(t)=t\big(1+\tfrac12\sin(1/t)\big)/\big(1+\tfrac12\sin1\big)$, continuous with $h(0)=0$, $h(1)=1$, and not of
bounded variation (Figure~\ref{fig:witness}) ($\int|h'|$ diverges at $0$). Let $\LR=\bracket{I_h}{\E}$. \axP, \axN{} and \axLI{} are clear;
$I_h$ is norm continuous (survival functions converge off a countable set, and dominated convergence applies)
and additive on simple comonotone positions by the layer computation, hence comonotonic additive, so \axCM{}
holds, and with it \axPe{} and \axR. The implied risk measure is $I_h$, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:bv} \axRC{} would force $h$ to be of bounded
variation. So \axRC{} fails and no pair in $\Hset_1^2$ represents $\LR$.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[Some regularity is indispensable]
\axP, \axN, \axCM{} and \axLI{} alone do not force continuity. Using a Hamel basis one obtains
$\rho=\E+\varphi\circ I_k$ with $k(t)=t(1-t)$ (so that $I_k$ vanishes on constants and is cash invariant) and
$\varphi$ additive but discontinuous. Then $\bracket{\rho}{\E}$ satisfies \axP, \axN, \axCM{} and \axLI, hence \axPe{} and \axR, and is not of
the form in Theorem~\ref{thm:A}. This witness uses the axiom of choice; all the others are constructive.
\end{remark}

\begin{example}[Independence of \axRM{} in Theorem~\ref{thm:B}]
Let $h(t)=t+\tfrac14\sin2\pi t\in\Hset_1$ (Figure~\ref{fig:witness}), so $h(0.4)\approx0.547>h(0.6)\approx0.453$, and
$\LR=\bracket{I_h}{\E}$. All other axioms hold (\axRC{} by the Lipschitz bound), but the implied risk measure
$I_h$ is not monotone, and no distortion pair represents $\LR$ because the pair is unique.
\end{example}

Axioms (A0) and (A1) define $\rho$ and $\Pi$ on $\Xp\setminus N_0$, through which the remaining axioms are stated, so they
are not treated as independent axioms. Two examples illustrate the identification they provide. If $\LR_0\equiv1$,
(A0) fails and $(\mathrm{id},\mathrm{id})$ and $(\sqrt\cdot,\sqrt\cdot)$ both represent $\LR_0$. If $\LR_0=(C_h/C_g)^3$ with
distortions $h\ne g$ and $g>0$, then $N_0$ is that of $C_h/C_g$, but (A1) fails, because the cash line has a pole of
order three at $-C_g(X)$ for $X\notin N_0$.

\begin{remark}[Atomlessness]
Atomlessness is used to attain every $p\in[0,1]$ as $P(A)$. On a space with atoms, $(h,g)$ is identified only
on the range of $P$.
\end{remark}
